A personal blog has been something I've wanted to start for a very long time now. Over the years, I've felt more and more number of reasons to start to accumulate in the snowball as it gained speed and size. Which brings us to this point in time.

## Why I want to start a personal blog

I'll enumerate my reasons for wanting to start a blog with a plain old list, in no particular order:

1. I want to start *producing* more. I'm a prolific consumer of content - long form blogs, podcasts, newsletters, videos, etc. I find myself consuming a lot and forgetting or not really fully digesting all the content I've consumed. Writing up reviews, recaps, and learnings from these consumptions will help me balance out how much I consume vs. produce. `Aim to read a lot.` and `Go deep on things. Become an expert.` are some pieces of [advice](https://patrickcollison.com/advice) from Stripe's CEO Patrick Collison, which remain timeless.

2. It's been said time and again - the best way to learn something is to teach it to others. I think a pre-requisite for even being able to teach something well is to first synthesize what you've learnt in your own words. This is the process that many greats follow - including Warren Buffet, who has always aimed to be remembered as a teacher before an investor.

This blog serves as a medium to synthesize my ideas, so that I can better teach, explain, and debate them.

3. In Herman's blog article titled [Observations on 6 years of journaling](https://herman.bearblog.dev/years-of-journaling/), he explicitly calls out how his long term habit of journaling has had an interesting side effect - that he gained better memory and more structured thoughts. I often find myself forgetting most of what I read (which is natural!) - but I'd still like to retain and internalize the 10-20% most impactful details from each source that I read. Additionally, being able to think clearly and speak articulately is another personal goal of mine.

4. Writing makes you a better speaker - this is another observation by Herman in his article above. He mentions that one you've articulated out your thoughts in writing, speaking them out in a structured way is a lot more straightforward since all of the hard untangling work has already been done.

5. Produce something _outside_ of work. With AI changing how jobs and careers look like, culture fit, breadth + depth of knowledge, and taste are starting to become some of the primary signals for hiring. In his recent article [Hunger beats tenure: Advice for junior engineers](https://felixrieseberg.com/hunger-beats-tenure-advice-for-junior-engineers/), Felix Rieseberg at Anthropic talks about how he's laying the traditional coding interview to rest. In terms of hiring, he mentions that 
> I’m scanning resumes and portfolios for little prototypes that exhibit taste, empathy, and principled thinking.
I think this is going to become more common across the industry, and I'd like for this blog to be a reflection of that.

## Discomforts

I have to admit that despite all the strong motivations I've mentioned above, I feel discomfort. They are mainly because of the following reasons:

- No one is going to want to read this, that I think that my thoughts are more important than they really are
- I'm going to abandon it after a few weeks
- I definitely don't want to "publicize" it

The best I can do for now is persist and see how things shape up.

## How I'm going to structure this blog

I'm going to KISS (Keep It Simple, Stupid) -
- One section for writings
- One section for reflections on content I've consumed (books, podcasts, articles, whatever)
- One section for notes - for concise notes on learnings and takeaways (idea stolen from Arpit's [blog](https://arpitbhayani.me/notes/))

Let's see how it goes.
