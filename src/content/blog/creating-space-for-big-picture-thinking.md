## Creating space for big picture thinking

Over the past couple of years, I've gradually come to the realization that I spend most of my days in this "focused, autopilot" mode. Which essentially means I spend my time focused on following my routine, and otherwise moving reactively to immediate events happening around me. 

In such a mental state, the default state is to allow work to consume quite a good chunk of my time and mental space. After all, there's always an infinite number of "urgent" and important things to do when it comes to work. Chipping away at them gives a definite sense of productivity, and being someone who prides himself on his work ethic - it feels aligned with who I am.

You certainly need to spend a fair portion of your day in a focused, productive mode where you knock off tasks - work as well as personal. The problem is when the focus you are putting starts to diverge away from the _true_ direction you'd want to put it into -- the direction that you would have come up with if you had sufficiently balanced your hours with big-picture thinking.

## Big-picture thinking

My definition of big-picture thinking is going to be refined over the coming few weeks. As of now, I'd start with some examples of focused vs. big-picture thinking:

Scenario 1: Building a feature at work:
- Focused: I need to deliver this feature on time. I need to refine the inter-service communication contract and implement the outbox pattern for publishing events. The dependency diagram of this system is getting complex, let me sketch it out and ensure I've covered all the edge cases.
  - Big-Picture: Is this the right feature to focus on? If we tweak this feature in this specific to simplify dev effort by 50%, will users still be happy? Am I working in the right team? Am I working in the right company and country to meet what I truly prize?

Scenario 2: Finding a long-term partner
- Focused: I need to create my Hinge profile and stay active there. I'll make sure I optimize my pictures and prompts to maximize matches and dates.
  - Big-Picture: What are the ideal qualities I look for in a partner? How do I optimize my demographics (ref: Mark Manson's Models) to meet a high volume of women of my type? What are some avenues that overlap with my interests + serve to put myself out there and meet more women - Social fitness events, acting classes, etc.?

## Conditions necessary to allow big-picture thinking to flourish

First, as Cal Newport puts it in the short essay *The Deep Life*, cultivating a deep life requires 4 parts:
- community (family, friends, etc.),
- craft (work and quality leisure),
- constitution (health), and
- contemplation (matters of the soul).

All of these are also necessary for creating space for big thinking. 

- Health is the pre-requisite
- Community is beneficial for psychological equanimity

You need to carve out hours in your day which are reserved for non-focused thinking. Even within your focused-thinking sessions (up to 8 hours a day including breaks), you need to be taking mini-breaks where you let your mind just wander, be bored if necessary, or do something you enjoy without the pressures of being productive.

You need to bring yourself out of survival mode too, for big-picture thinking to truly flourish. Being in survival mode puts you in a hyper-focused mode because that's what worked from an evolutionary perspective. It however, crowds out the space in your mind from big-picture thinking. 

Personally, it definitely *feels* different in my head when I've spent a lot of time in focus mode. Accessing the big picture thinking, wit, and creativity become a lot harder.

Conversely, things like chilling out, socializing, etc. help *slowly* restore the big picture thinking - the degree of restoration required being proportionate to how long I've spent in survival mode.

## It's not just the obvious things that put you in survival mode

Work, personal distress are a few obvious things that narrow your focus and put you in survival mode. However, there's a lot of other subtle things that can cause this. Anything that restricts energy to the brain can cause the same effect, including but not limited to:
- Insufficient sleep.
- Involuntarily holding your breath or breathing shallow-ly which subsequently reduces oxygen supply to the brain (try to be mindful of this, I realised I was doing it more often than I realized).
  - Especially during taxing or heavy-focus activities like being nervous, playing the guitar, etc.
- Dietary intolerances - everyone who knows me has head me talk about how dairy gives me brain fog. The gut and brain are quite interlinked.
- Small sadnesses or stressors (which are just misalignments) adding up over the days.
- Poor sleep quality despite sufficient quantity.


## My plans for getting myself out of consistent survival mode and creating space for big-picture thinking

As of now, I'm just happy that I've allowed myself some space to think big-picture - which has lead to the writing of the draft of this article. If I reflect back on things that have helped me feel more clear minded, they are:
- Watching Minecraft, Dota, Valorant, Mr. Beast content
- Spending time in nature
- Eating something yum (not the best habit)
- Spending time away from screens
- Exercise - either light-moderate (eg: long walk) or heavy (eg: lifting session) based on what I feel like
- Talking to someone - about whatever's on my mind or even just nothing at all
- Doomscrolling - only if it's brainrot content, ideally nothing serious.

Plan: start the day with diffuse time, spend time in the afternoons or evenings.
